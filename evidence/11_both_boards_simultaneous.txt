Both boards captured simultaneously, 2026-09-07.

A single PowerShell loop polled COM3 and COM4 in the same process, timestamping
each line as it arrived. Every other log in this directory is a single-port
capture, so this is the only evidence that shows one packet at both ends of the
radio link with a common clock.

What it demonstrates:

  - The same sequence number leaving Board 1 and arriving at Board 2. seq=12 is
    acknowledged on the node at 13:20:24.757 and logged by the gateway at
    13:20:24.758 - one millisecond, which is the ESP-NOW round trip including
    the link-layer ack.
  - "OK (link-layer ack received)" on the node is corroborated by an actual
    arrival on the gateway, rather than being taken on trust.
  - node1_sequence in the MQTT payload matches the seq the node just sent, so
    edge aggregation is carrying the current reading, not a cached one.
  - invalid=0, wrong_sender=0, duplicates=0 throughout.

Context: this capture was taken immediately after recovering from a channel
roam. The gateway had moved to channel 6 while the node was pinned to 1, and
the first attempt at this capture recorded the failure instead - the node
reporting "FAILED (no ack)" with total_fail=14 while the gateway published its
own readings normally with node1_online=false. That failing capture is the
opening of this file's story and the reason section 9 treats the roam as a
standing property of the network rather than an incident.
